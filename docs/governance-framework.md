# Governance framework

BAPIL is a **proposal**. None of the bodies below has been constituted, no charter has been adopted, no host institution has been selected and no MoU has been signed. The authoritative description is the Technical Blueprint, Chapter 5 (institutional design), Chapter 13 (responsible AI assurance), Annexes E to G (draft charter and terms of reference) and Annex S with Table 64 (status and limits of each body).

## 1. Proposed institutional model

Option C of the Blueprint: a **university-anchored lab** working under an MoU with the ICT Division. The host institution provides the legal home for staff, funds and data; BAPIL has no regulatory role, and the authorities created under the data-protection, data-management and cyber-security laws keep their full powers over its activities.

## 2. Proposed bodies

| Body | Role | Status | Main limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steering Board | 7 to 9 members; approves plans, budgets, partnerships and senior appointments | Proposed; created by the MoU and host agreement; members to be nominated (Decision D4) | No statutory power; cannot override an ERRC refusal or the law; the host approves the financial framework |
| Ethics and Risk Review Committee (ERRC) | 5 to 7 members, a majority external; approves, conditions, refuses or suspends tier T2 and T3 work at gates G1, G2 and G4; a refusal is binding | Proposed; established by the charter; members selected by open call through an independent nominations panel (D4) | Its decisions bind the host only as far as the host’s statutes and the host agreement allow; legal review before signing (unresolved item U15) |
| Technical Architecture Board (TAB) | Approves designs at G1, models and major dependencies; keeps architecture decision records public | Proposed; internal technical body under the Director | No authority outside technical design; external reviewers declare commercial interests |
| Director | Single point of accountability; may stop any pilot | Proposed post; not appointed | Host employee appointed by open competition and confirmed by the Steering Board |

If the independent AI oversight committee proposed in the draft national AI policy is created by an Act of Parliament, BAPIL would register its systems with it and follow its decisions; the ERRC would continue only as an internal review.

## 3. Assurance system

* **Risk tiers T0 to T3** set by a questionnaire (Annex B), combining the categories of the draft national AI policy with the impact-level logic of Canada’s Algorithmic Impact Assessment.
* **Lifecycle gates G0 to G5** with checklists (Annex J); no deployment beyond advisory use (class A) without a G4 decision (Decision D6).
* **Documentation:** model cards, datasheets, a public transparency register of every AI system, and a complaints route answered within thirty days.
* **Prohibited uses** written into the draft charter (Article 4): social scoring; mass or indiscriminate biometric identification or surveillance; profiling of political opinions, religious beliefs or similar protected characteristics; deceptive synthetic media depicting real people; manipulation that exploits vulnerabilities. Out of scope for the pilots (Section 12.9): monitoring of named individuals or groups, prediction of individual behaviour, eligibility or enforcement decisions, and publishing AI-generated text as official government communication.
